\title{Reliability via Verified Preference: Breaking the Verified-Replay Ceiling on Single-Attempt Reliability}
\author{cmohsinm}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Language models often \emph{can} reach a correct solution (high coverage, pass@$k$) yet fail to
\emph{select} it in one shot (low single-attempt reliability, pass@1). We study \textbf{Reliability via
Verified Preference (RVP)}: after rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT), a single decoupled
preference pass on the model's \emph{own} verifier-labeled correct/incorrect samples, which raises the
correct-vs-incorrect logit margin by suppressing incorrect modes and thereby lifts pass@1 at
\emph{constant coverage} --- reliability by selection, not new capability. Across Qwen2.5-Math
$\{1.5\text{B},7\text{B}\}$ bases and six math benchmarks, RVP delivers headroom-gated gains of $+.04$
to $+.21$ pass@1; the improvement amortizes inference-time selection into the weights (RVP at a single
sample matches base majority-vote self-consistency at $\sim\!8$ samples), and a layer-wise analysis
localizes it to late-layer margin ascent. We are deliberately careful about what is \emph{not} shown: a
matched-compute second imitation round (ReST) is statistically competitive on pass@1, so verified
negatives are not \emph{necessary} for the accuracy gain --- but exact probability-mass accounting reveals
a \emph{mechanistic dissociation}: only RVP widens the aggregate correct-vs-incorrect margin, so the two
methods reach a common endpoint by different internal routes. RVP's contribution is therefore a cheap,
single-pass selection operator together with a \emph{predictive, headroom-gated account} of when verified
post-training converts coverage into single-attempt reliability --- and where it does not (near-ceiling
instruct models; catastrophically on general RLHF-chat bases). We correct two theoretical claims from an
earlier draft (positive-only cross-entropy does exert diffuse softmax pressure on incorrect logits; the
GRPO shortfall is a finite-budget, not an impossibility, statement) and report all nulls and scope
boundaries honestly.
\end{abstract}
